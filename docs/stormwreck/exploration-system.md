## Show, Don’t tell Principle

### Story that should inspire world building:

“Stormwreck Isle was born when three metallic dragons, followers of Bahamut, defeated the pow erful red dragon Sharruth and imprisoned her beneath the ocean floor. Sharruth’s fury and the volcanic activity caused by her imprisonment fractured the land and forced it to rise around her prison, forming the island. Since then, chromatic and metallic dragons have been drawn to the power of the place and have fought numerous battles there, leaving bones, ruins, and scars across the landscape. The deaths of so many dragons, combined with Sharruth’s essence still emanating from her prison, have infused the island with draconic energy, causing earthquakes, magical manifestations, unusual creatures, and phenomena associated with the different types of dragons.”The world building and exploration elements should reflect that story.Locations of Stormwreck Isle:

Dragon’s Rest: This is where players start the adventure, and their Safe Heaven for narrative and gameplay purposes.

Seagrove Case: A case where a myconid community lives.

Compass Rose Shipwreck: The wreck of a ship named Compass Rose.

Clifftop Observatory: The observatory where Sparkrender is planning and executing their schemes.

Places of Power (3 locations): Locations where the power of dragons is concentrated in such a way that if you spend time communing with it, you are able to harness the power of a dragon.

## Combat System elements:

Permanent Injuries: When a character reaches 10% of its total health, a permanent injury from a Permanent Injury table is rolled. The character acquires this injury permanently even if its hp is recovered.

## Exploration System of Stormwreck Isle:

It’s a system that aims to transform the traveling part of the game into a playable experience. This is done through counting time through turns, the player has to declare activities when exploring an area of the map which will make them face travelling events.

The Map of the Stormwreck Isle will have 3 different regions where the encounters will act differently. Each region tells a different story through their table entries and will have a difficulty associated with it. The encounters in one region can be harder or easier than others. It’s up to the players through investigation to find out where the safest or dangerous passage through the Isle is. Those regions are:

Region A: A low difficult region where in its northeast part, the presence of undead creatures is its main characteristic and its west part has one Dragon Power location. Dragon’s Rest and The wreck of the compass rose can be found here.

Region B: A mid difficulty region located west of the Isle, it's where the Sea Grove cave is located, where the myconics are present. There is also a Dragon Power location here.

Region C: A highly difficult region located southeast of the Isle, this is where the Clifftop Observatory is located. There is also a Dragon Power location here.

## Travel System:

This system includes ways of traveling through Stormwreck Isle. The party members will be treated as an expedition. Each member of the expedition will engage in one or more activities. These activities will prevent bad things from happening during the travel or grant them some kind of advantage if they perform it. As they pass through the hexes they will be thrown at events that will require them to make checks, those checks will be based or not on the activities they are performing. Each event has its own stakes, and description of what happens if they fail or succeed the check.

## Environmental Hazards

Environmental Hazards are any natural, magic or individual-caused condition that threaten safety, health or survival of living organisms and their surroundings. The Isle is full of them. And to travel safely under those conditions are necessary for the expedition to perform one or more kinds of activities to neutralize it. Below you can see a list of environmental hazards that populates the Isle:

| --- | --- |

## Other Activities

Depending on the area the expedition is passing through, there might be environmental hazards throughout the way. For example, Dense Vegetation on a forest, Unstable Cliffs or Strong Clashing Waves through a coast, Agitated Sea in an ocean area. All of those required one or more activities to be performed by characters moving through that area. Usually the logic is if they do not perform the activity, they are punished in some way or/and depending on creativity if they perform it they gain something or some advantage. In either way, what they need to perform, whether they gain or lose, all should be explained by the Environmental Activity description. Examples of Environmental Activities are explained further below.

## Travel Events:

These are challenges that pop up during the trip. They can be hex fixed or randomly generated. Ideally they are based on checks of the activities the party is performing but that’s not obligatory. Below there are some examples of travel events:

The Misdirecting Vale: The expedition encounters themselves in terrain that has a really low level, they get surrounded by high terrain. The terrain can make the expedition lose direction. The player that is performing the Navigation activity makes a check. In a fail, the expedition ends up going in a different direction for the rest of the hour and is lost. In a success the party follows the path it wanted to follow. If an expedition is lost it can start the next hour by making a Navigation check, if it fails it stays lost and moves into a random direction, if it succeeds it goes to the path it wanted and its not lost anymore.

The Ambush: The expedition passes through an area that a big predator is hunting. The players performing the Watch activity make a check. In a fail, the expedition is completely ignorant of the predator's presence and gets ambushed by it. A combat unfolds. In a success, the expedition gets aware of the predator’s presence and gets to react to it before it has time to ambush them.

The Particularly Difficult Terrain: The expedition passes through a terrain that is a particularly difficult area that is dangerous to go through with a vehicle/mount. The player(s) performing the Piloting/Riding need to make a check. If they fail the vehicle or ride gets damaged. In a success they pass through safely.

Big Wave Crush: The expedition passes through a cost type of terrain and a big destructive wave comes on their way, to predict where the wave trajectory will crash exactly to avoid it one or more players need to make a check of Coastering. The players who fail this check need to make a dexterity saving through or receive damage.

Dangerous Hacking and Slash: The expedition passes through a kind of vegetation that needs to be carefully hacked to pass through as this vegetation is made of poisoned vines, or mushrooms, or spines wipes. The player performing Bushwhacking needs to make a check. In a fail, all players get damaged by the poisoned/spined vegetation.

Unstable Cliffs: The expedition passes through cliffs that have unstable ground and can just fall apart causing members of the expedition to fall from high heights. All players performing Scrambling need to make a check. If they fail they fall from cliffs heights and get damaged.

Tricky Unstable Ground: The expedition passes through false stable ground. The player performing Terrain Probing must make a check to identify the fake stable ground. In a failure the “not performing" section of the Terrain Probing activity happens.

Thick Toxic Gas Cloud: The expedition passes through an area that a moving gas cloud can engulf them. The player performing the Fumarole Navigation can make a check to try to quickly move the expedition away from the cloud. In a failure the expedition spends too much time inside the cloud and gets poison damage.

Massive Draconic Energy Surge: The expedition passes through an area of super draconic energy instability that takes them by surprise. The player performing Arcane Stabling needs to make a check to try to quickly negate the massive effect of this super burst of energy. In a failure the expedition is the target of a massive breath weapon that comes out of nowhere. All must succeed in a dexterity saving throw or take 5d6 of a randomic type of energy (acid, lightning, fire, poison or cold).

Big Wave Coming: The expedition passes through an area that a massive wave is passing through. The players performing Balancing need to make a check to try not to fall from the boat. Whoever fails falls into water and is taken by sea current and winds away from the boat. Getting back to the boat by swimming takes an athletics check. In a failure the player gets back to the boat anyways but also takes one exhaustion level.

## How to run the hex traveling

The players starts in a hex:

Step 1: DM asks players which direction and speed they go.

Step 2: DM projects what the players will encounter along the way. The DM prepares. The DM moves them hex by hex until they see something worth describing on the map, they stumble upon an event on the map or they move up to their speed.

Step 3: If they see something interesting, the DM describes it to them. If they stumble upon an event, the DM runs the event. If they move up to their speed, increase time (1 hour).

Step 4: When the description ends, the event ends or the time increase is done, if players are still alive, the DM goes back to Step 1.

## How the Environmental Hazard Areas Activity Impacts Travel System

Player roles should impact on how the environmental hazards should be engaged. Letting them make choices on what activities they should engage in that should increase or decrease their odds of succeeding on traversing the hexes with the environmental hazards.

How can we implement this? Simple, every turn on exploration mode, the players should choose an activity to be doing while traveling (this list of activities is inside the avernus folders). Each environmental hazard will require one or more activities that the players should engage in that will require them to make tests, just like the regular activities. Depending on the Environmental Hazard players will have to divide their attention in order to not fail the tests that it requires in order for things to not go badly. For example: If the players are passing through the Exceptionally Strong Waves EH their Atletism and Acrobatics test will be an activity that each player will need to do in order to not suffer the consequences of the EH. But if they need to also navigate, or watch, or search for food or a location, their attention will probably be divided. Some of the players will have to take some tests at disadvantage. Choosing who will do a certain activity, will be the game loop of the exploration. Something like this: Players enter an area, DM explains the area, players choose activities, players choose the speed they are committed to traverse this round/turn, roll tests, deliver consequences.

Another example is the Hidden Sea Caves EH, in this case, they can choose one person to guide them to not step into the insatiable ground. So this specific test is super important as this can impact the whole party if it fails, so they will need to maybe choose the support activity in order to increase the chances of that activity to not fail. So, each activity required for the players to do, is described by the EH.
