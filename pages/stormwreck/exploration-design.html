01 / CONTEXT & GOAL

Make the island's history playable.

The document starts from the island's history of dragon conflict and magical energy. Its stated worldbuilding direction is to express that history through the environment and exploration. Dragon's Rest is the starting refuge; caves, a shipwreck, an observatory and proposed places of power provide destinations.

The design goal is to make the journey involve investigation, preparation and risk, with encounters that belong to this particular island.

03 / A DOCUMENTED TRADEOFF

Faster travel raises hazard difficulty.

One turn takes an hour. On foot or by rowboat, the draft limits travel to six hexes per turn. The speed table increases a hazard's check difficulty as the chosen pace rises.

ILLUSTRATIVE RULE COMPARISON

One hex: lower difficulty

The Strong Waves activity starts at DC 5. At one hex per turn, the modifier is +0, leaving the check at DC 5.

ILLUSTRATIVE RULE COMPARISON

Four to six hexes: higher difficulty

The same activity receives a +8 modifier, raising the check to DC 13. This compares the specified rules; it is not a playtest result.

This demonstrates an explicit relationship between pace and risk. Whether its costs create worthwhile route choices requires testing and consistent definitions of exposure and failure.

04 / EXPEDITION ACTIVITIES

Decide where to allocate attention.

NAVIGATEKeep direction

A character guides the expedition toward its intended destination.

WATCH OR FORAGEAddress other needs

Activities can seek warnings or supplies while the party moves.

SUPPORTHelp a teammate

The draft grants advantage to an ally's activity check.

Terrain can require additional activities. The document proposes divided attention and disadvantage when characters take on competing demands, but the exact capacity and allocation rules still need a single consistent specification.

05 / EXPLORATION MEETS COMBAT

Carry the terrain into the encounter.

DENSE UNDERGROWTH

Movement and visibility

During travel, failed passage checks slow progress. In combat, the terrain becomes difficult ground with vegetation blocking sight lines.

STRONG WAVES

A recurring environmental threat

During travel, each character must avoid the waves. On a battle map, a die determines the interval between incoming waves.

The same environmental identity affects two modes of play. This demonstrates a connection between exploration content and encounter conditions; it does not establish that either version has been balanced.

06 / CONTEXTUAL ENCOUNTERS

Generate situations, not only opponents.

The encounter framework combines region, terrain and whether the party is traveling or stationary. Creature generation adds posture, activity and draconic characteristics. A historical-feature generator provides traces such as dragon bones, battlefields or ruined temples.

Activities such as fleeing, scavenging or protecting something describe a situation the group can interpret. The dragon features and landscape traces tie those situations to the island's premise.

07 / DEVELOPMENT REVIEW

What needs another design pass.

The document demonstrates system relationships and concrete resolution rules. Its next revision should consolidate repeated hazard definitions, complete the environmental-activity section and define terms such as food units and exposure.

The five-person travel example also needs correction: support targets and the labels for characters making checks are inconsistent. The speed table includes a seven-plus tier although the stated travel modes cap speed at six. Encounter distributions and severity, including falls, exhaustion and permanent injuries, need explicit review and playtesting.

These are findings from reviewing version 1.2, not claims that a revised version has already been completed.
